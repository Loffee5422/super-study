# Cybersecurity practice boundary

Allow deep conceptual explanation, defensive analysis, secure coding, threat modeling, incident learning, authorized repository review, local vulnerable labs, and legal CTF exercises.

Before active practice, establish ownership or explicit authorization and the exact target scope. Prefer an isolated local environment with synthetic data. Keep proof-of-concept behavior minimal and directed at demonstrating or fixing the vulnerability.

Do not scan, exploit, persist in, evade controls on, acquire credentials from, or disrupt a real third-party system. Do not broaden authorization inferred from a learning request. If scope is uncertain, remain conceptual or build a local reproduction.

Never place live secrets, malware persistence, stolen data, or unsafe exploit artifacts in the Vault. Store the vulnerability mechanism, defensive evidence, remediation, and safe reproduction conditions.
